# The soundtrack

**Off by default.** Feeds autoplay muted, the video works silent, and there
is no voiceover (the on-screen text is the script). Turn sound on only when
the user asks for music or sound, or for a launch or screen video when the
user agrees to the suggestion.

## What it is

A score synthesised in the page by Web Audio and rendered once, offline, at
48 kHz for the cut's exact length:

```json
"audio": { "score": "pulse", "bpm": 112 }
```

- `pulse`: a soft kick on each beat at `bpm` (default 112) and a quiet hat
  on the off-beat.
- `ambient`: a low pad that changes chord with each beat.

Both add a sub note on each beat's downbeat, a short noise sweep on each
transition and a tick when a counter lands. The effects sit 6 dB under the
score, and the whole track is normalised to a conservative level with a peak
ceiling.

With a score on, **every beat's words start on a musical beat**: the compiler
holds the beat before a little longer to get there. Durations printed by
`check-video.js` already include this.

Preview plays the same rendered track as the export, so what you hear is
what ships. Export encodes AAC in MP4 and Opus in WebM. A browser without an
audio encoder exports a silent video and says so in its status line.

## Rights: say this at handover when sound is on

> The soundtrack is generated by code inside the file: original, with no
> third-party track or sample, so there's no licence to clear. A track you add
> yourself is under that track's licence.

A user-supplied audio track is not in v1. If asked, say so and suggest adding
it in their editing app after export.
