# Five Ardens: a family car across a century

*Hallam Motor Museum · gallery guide, room 4 · published 12 March 2026*

The Arden family car has been built in Hallam since 1912. Room 4 shows five of them, one from each era of the company, parked in the order they were made.

The 1912 Arden Tourer was an open car: a wooden-framed body on a steel chassis, a folding cloth hood, and spoked wheels. Its 20 horsepower engine gave a top speed of about 60 km/h. The works built 8,200 Tourers.

By 1934 the Arden Saloon had a closed all-steel body with glass windows all round. It weighed 1,150 kg and reached 95 km/h.

The 1958 Arden Starliner is the car most visitors photograph: long, low, with chrome and rear fins. Its top speed was 150 km/h. The museum describes it as "one of the best-selling cars Hallam ever built", with 412,000 made.

The 1979 Arden Compact was a short hatchback with a 1.3-litre engine, using 6.4 litres of fuel per 100 km. It went on sale the year after fuel prices in the country doubled. The company's records do not say whether the two are linked.

The 2024 Arden Volt is electric. Arden says its range is up to 480 km on a full charge, and its top speed is 170 km/h.

In 1912 a Tourer cost about 2,400 hours of a factory worker's wages, by the museum's estimate. A Volt costs an estimated 1,100 hours today.

Arden expects its next model, planned for 2030, could charge to 80% in about 10 minutes.

"We kept one of everything," says curator Ines Varga. "You can walk a century in forty steps."
