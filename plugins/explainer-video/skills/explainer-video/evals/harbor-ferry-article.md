# Kestrel Bay ferries carry record crowds — or close to it

*The Kestrel Bay Courier · Local news · 30 September 2026 · By Nadia Okonkwo*

Kestrel Bay's harbour ferries had their busiest year in well over a decade,
according to figures published on Monday by the Kestrel Bay Transport Board.

The board's annual report shows ridership rose 41% on the previous year, to
1.9 million trips in the twelve months to the end of August. It is the highest
annual total since 2009, when the service carried just over 2 million
passengers before the old Pier Road terminal closed for rebuilding.

Weekday commuter sailings made up 63% of all trips, the report says, while
weekend and holiday sailings accounted for the rest.

"Every one of those trips is someone who chose the water over the bridge, and
we intend to keep earning that choice," said Tomasz Reyes, the board's director
of marine services.

Looking ahead, the board estimates ridership could reach 2.5 million trips a
year by 2029 if current trends continue, though the report cautions that the
projection depends on fuel prices and on the delivery of two new vessels.

In March the board opened a new route linking Gull Point with the city
centre, the first new crossing in eight years. Gull Point residents had
campaigned for a direct service since 2019.

Separately, the city council's budget committee agreed in June to freeze
ferry fares at their 2025 level until at least April 2027. Single adult fares
remain £2.80.

The report also notes that average punctuality slipped slightly, to 94% of
sailings departing within three minutes of schedule, down from 96% the year
before.

The Transport Board will publish its next annual report in September 2027.
