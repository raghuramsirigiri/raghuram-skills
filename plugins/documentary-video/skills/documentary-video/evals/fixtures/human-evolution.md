# Seven million years in a few pages: how we became human

*A short explainer written as a test document from commonly cited figures.
Dates are approximate and revised as new fossils are found.*

## Walking first

The human story starts in Africa. *Sahelanthropus tchadensis*, found in Chad,
lived about 7 million years ago and may be one of the earliest members of
the human lineage, though that is still debated.

Walking upright came long before big brains. At Laetoli in Tanzania, a trail
of footprints pressed into volcanic ash about 3.66 million years ago shows
individuals walking on two legs with a human-like stride. "Lucy", a skeleton
of *Australopithecus afarensis* found in Ethiopia, lived about 3.2 million
years ago. She stood a little over 1 metre tall, walked upright and still
climbed trees, and her species had a brain of roughly 400 to 500 cubic
centimetres, about the size of a chimpanzee's.

## Tools and a new body

The oldest known stone tools, from Lomekwi in Kenya, are about 3.3 million
years old. By about 1.9 million years ago *Homo erectus* had appeared, with
long legs and a body shaped much like ours, and a brain that grew over its
long history to around 1,000 cubic centimetres. *Homo erectus* made
teardrop-shaped hand axes and was the first human species known to leave
Africa: its fossils at Dmanisi in Georgia are about 1.8 million years old.

Early evidence of fire comes from Wonderwerk Cave in South Africa, about 1
million years ago. Regular use of fire became widespread by around 400,000
years ago.

## Other humans

Neanderthals lived across Europe and western Asia from about 400,000 until
about 40,000 years ago. They were stocky and strong, built for the cold, and
their brains averaged around 1,500 cubic centimetres, as large as ours or
larger. They made fine stone tools and buried some of their dead.

## Our species

The oldest known fossils of *Homo sapiens*, from Jebel Irhoud in Morocco,
are about 300,000 years old. The modern human brain averages about 1,350
cubic centimetres.

Most people living outside Africa today descend from a dispersal that left
Africa roughly 60,000 to 70,000 years ago. People reached Australia by about
50,000 years ago and the Americas by at least 15,000 years ago.

On the way they met Neanderthals. Today, people of non-African descent carry
about 1 to 2 percent Neanderthal DNA.
